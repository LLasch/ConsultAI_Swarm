# Case Brief: Water Utility Company

**Company:** German-owned water utility (Northeast United States)
**Industry:** utilities
**Source:** Haas_2006.pdf (pages 50–51)
**Tags:** utilities, regulatory, financial_metrics, capacity_utilization, geographic_expansion, revenue_growth

## Current Situation

The water utility is headquartered in the Northeast US and serves as the exclusive water provider in the majority of the region. It measures performance using EVA, which equals Net Operating Profits minus (WACC × Capital Asset Base). The company has relatively flat revenues over 12-18 months from residential and commercial consumption-based billing. Costs are stable, pricing is heavily regulated and cannot be significantly altered, and the company has excess capacity to serve additional customers. The utility has faced socio-political resentment regarding its German ownership and history of acquiring local US companies.

## Key Challenges

A German-owned water utility operating in the Northeast US is currently operating at negative Economic Value Added (EVA) and needs to improve financial performance. The company must determine how to increase EVA to zero and eventually turn it positive, given regulatory constraints on pricing and flat revenues.

## Discussion Questions

- Given that operating profits have a larger effect on EVA than the capital asset base component, what are the primary levers to improve operating profits given regulatory constraints on pricing?
- How could the water utility leverage its excess capacity to grow its customer base, and what barriers (regulatory, competitive, socio-political) would need to be addressed?
- Should the company consider diversification strategies such as bottled water distribution or geographic expansion, and what would be the cost-benefit analysis of each approach?
