# Case Brief: So Fresh and So Clean

**Company:** So Fresh and So Clean
**Industry:** distribution
**Source:** Fuqua-Case-book-2014.pdf (pages 206–215)
**Tags:** cost_reduction, sales_force_optimization, post_acquisition_integration, distribution, margin_compression, organizational_efficiency

---

## Current Situation

So Fresh and So Clean is the leading player in the U.S. cleaning supply distribution market with 20% market share, serving restaurants, hospitals, schools, and food service providers with chemicals, cleaning supplies, uniforms, and equipment. The company completed acquisitions of a $3.3B revenue competitor in 2010 and a $3.1B revenue competitor in 2012. The broader industry has grown only 2% annually over the past five years and margins are relatively aligned across competitors, indicating the problem is company-specific rather than market-wide. Despite strong market position, profitability has steadily declined post-acquisition.

## Key Challenges

So Fresh and So Clean, a national cleaning supply distributor and industry leader, has experienced declining net margins from 3.1% in 2009 to 2.4% in 2013 despite acquiring two major competitors. The CEO is concerned about the company's long-term viability and has commissioned a diagnostic to identify the source of margin erosion. The core issue stems from an ineffective and under-optimized sales force that was not rationalized following post-acquisition integration.

## Decision

The company recommended implementing a new sales model that would hire entry-level account coordinators to handle administrative tasks, freeing senior sales representatives to spend more time selling and increase customer account coverage, while reducing overall workforce headcount.
