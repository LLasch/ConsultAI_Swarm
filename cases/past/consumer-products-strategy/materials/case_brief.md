# Case Brief: Consumer Products Strategy

**Company:** Large multinational consumer products company (unnamed)
**Industry:** retail
**Source:** Fuqua-Case-book-2014.pdf (pages 236–243)

## Current Situation

The client is a large multinational consumer products company with a premium product strategy, historically selling high-end products across grocery stores, convenience stores, and mass retailers. US demographic analysis shows low-income households (at or below poverty level) are growing twice as quickly as other consumer segments. The company has identified potential opportunities in product categories including shampoo, cold medicine, diapers, and baby food, but must evaluate market attractiveness and competitive positioning.

## Key Challenges

A premium consumer products company with operations in 200+ countries is considering whether to enter the rapidly growing low-income household segment in the US, a demographic expanding twice as fast as other segments. The company has never targeted low-income consumers and lacks a strategy for this market, requiring analysis of opportunity and risks.

## Decision

Pursue a low-income strategy focused on diapers by reducing package size from 40 to 20 diapers while maintaining price per diaper, resulting in a $10 price point that is lower than competitors.

## Outcome

Projected to generate an additional $25M in revenue from the low-income segment by entering the diaper category with smaller, more affordable package sizes.

## Discussion Questions

- Should the client enter the low-income segment, and if so, which product categories offer the best opportunity?
- What distribution and pricing tactics should the client deploy to successfully reach low-income consumers who primarily shop in smaller local stores?
- What are the key risks of pursuing a low-income strategy, including potential brand dilution and cannibalization of existing premium products?
