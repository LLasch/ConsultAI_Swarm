# Case Brief: Institutional Asset Manager Fees

**Industry:** finance

## Current Situation

Institutional asset managers generate revenues by charging clients a percentage fee based on assets under management (AUM). Clients range from pension funds (CalPers, TIAA-CREF) to foundations and universities. The fee structure is stepped, with larger clients receiving lower percentage fees but generating higher absolute revenues at similar servicing costs. The case focuses on the institutional investor business segment rather than retail/individual investor divisions.

## Key Challenges

The client, a profitable institutional asset manager, is charging lower average fees on assets under management than industry competitors. Despite strong profitability, the company is leaving money on the table and needs to understand why their fee structure is below industry average.

## Decision

Implement pricing discipline and monitoring systems: (1) diagnose the root causes of excessive discounting by tracking which salespeople/account executives are offering discounts and to which client types; (2) establish caps on maximum discount levels (estimated around 30%); (3) align discount strategy to prioritize larger clients; (4) implement tracking and monitoring systems; (5) redesign sales incentives to discourage excessive discounting.

## Outcome

Not disclosed. The case indicates the goal is to raise the company's average fee to at least industry average through systematic changes to discount practices and sales incentive structures, but specific financial results are not provided.

## Discussion Questions

- Why is the client's average fee lower than the industry average despite being highly profitable?
- How should the company restructure its discount strategy to improve overall profitability and fee realization?
