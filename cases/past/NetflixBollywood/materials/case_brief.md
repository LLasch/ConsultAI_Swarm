# Case Brief: Netflix Goes to Bollywood

## 1. Client Overview

**Netflix, Inc.**
- **Founded**: 1997 by Marc Randolph and Reed Hastings
- **Evolution**: Mail-order DVD rental → Streaming video on demand (SVOD) leader
- **Mission**: Shift power to viewers by providing content libraries accessible anytime, anywhere, without commercial interruptions
- **Global Position**: World's leading SVOD provider with streaming services in 190 countries

## 2. Current Situation

### Financial Position (2019)
- **Enterprise value**: $157 billion
- **Global subscribers**: 167 million paid subscribers
- **International penetration**: 60% of subscribers outside US/Canada
- **Content investment**: $15 billion in original programming (2019), planned $17 billion (2020)
- **Projected spending**: $26 billion annually by 2028

### Operations
- Produces more original content than any TV network or Hollywood studio
- Average viewing increased 4x between 2010-2015
- 40% of Americans binge-watch weekly (2019)

### Market Context - India Focus
- **India subscribers**: ~2 million (vs Disney+ 8 million in 5 months)
- **Market size**: 1.3 billion population
- **Investment strategy**: Fastest rate of increase vs any other market
- **Pricing**: Mobile-only plan at $2.85/month (July 2019)
- **Target**: "Next 100 million subscribers" from India

## 3. Key Challenges

1. **Intense competitive pressure**: Disney+, Apple TV+, AT&T, and Comcast launching competing services backed by $215 billion in acquisitions
2. **Content dependency risk**: ~40% of Netflix viewing time comes from content licensed from three major competitors
3. **India market penetration**: Despite 4-year presence, only 2 million subscribers vs Disney+'s 8 million in 5 months
4. **Price sensitivity mismatch**: Indian market prefers ad-subsidized low fees vs Netflix's premium ad-free model
5. **Cultural/language barriers**: Current focus on English/Hindi content while majority prefer local language content
6. **Strategic constraints**: Historical commitments to premium pricing, no M&A, and no advertising may limit India success

## 5. Financial Highlights

### Content Investment Trajectory
- 2013: $100 million (House of Cards)
- 2018: $13 billion
- 2019: $15 billion
- 2020 (planned): $17 billion
- 2028 (projected): $26 billion annually

### Subscriber Growth
- 2000: 300,000 subscribers
- 2002 IPO: 600,000+ subscribers
- 2014: ~40 million US streaming subscribers
- 2019: 167 million global paid subscribers

### Market Valuation Evolution
- 2006: $1.5 billion (vs Blockbuster's <$750 million)
- 2019: $157 billion enterprise value

## 6. Constraints

### Strategic Commitments
- **No advertising policy**: Historical pledge conflicts with ad-subsidized Indian market preferences
- **Premium pricing model**: $2.85 mobile-only plan still above local competitor pricing
- **No M&A strategy**: Organic growth approach may be too slow for India competition

### Market Realities
- **Content licensing vulnerability**: Major studios terminating contracts as they launch competing services
- **Infrastructure limitations**: India's price-sensitive market structure
- **Cultural requirements**: Need for local language content production capabilities

### Timeline Pressure
- Disney+ achieved 50 million subscribers in 5 months vs Netflix's 2 million in 4 years in India

## 7. Open Questions

### Critical Information Gaps
1. **Specific India financial performance**: Revenue per user, churn rates, content costs for local productions
2. **Competitor pricing analysis**: Detailed comparison of Disney+ Hotstar and local player pricing strategies
3. **Local content investment**: Current vs required spending on regional language programming
4. **Distribution partnerships**: Potential telecom or device manufacturer relationships in India
5. **Regulatory constraints**: Government content regulations, foreign ownership rules
6. **Customer segmentation data**: Urban vs rural viewing patterns, language preferences by region
7. **Infrastructure costs**: Technology investment required for broader Indian market penetration

### Strategic Decision Points
- Should Netflix abandon its no-advertising policy for India?
- Is M&A activity necessary to acquire local content libraries and talent?
- Can the premium positioning be maintained while achieving mass market penetration?
