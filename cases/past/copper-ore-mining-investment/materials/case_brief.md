# Case Brief: Copper Ore Mining Investment

**Company:** Excavator and Drillhammer (two mining companies)
**Industry:** manufacturing
**Source:** Wharton_2010.pdf (pages 58–61)
**Tags:** joint_venture, mining, investment_valuation, negotiation, capital_allocation, manufacturing

---

## Current Situation

Excavator and Drillhammer are considering copper ore mining projects with 20-year mine lives. Excavator's site can support 1 million tonnes per annum with $35/tonne operating costs and $15/tonne operating profit. Drillhammer's site is less efficient at $53/tonne operating costs, resulting in a $3/tonne loss. A proposed joint venture at Excavator's site would operate at 2 million tonnes per annum with $30/tonne operating costs. Copper ore is priced at $50/tonne in the analysis.

## Key Challenges

Two copper ore mining companies must decide whether to invest independently in their respective mining sites or form a joint venture. Excavator can profitably operate alone, but Drillhammer's site is loss-making independently. The decision involves evaluating which investment structure creates the most value and how to allocate that value fairly between partners.

## Decision

Excavator should pursue the joint venture with Drillhammer rather than investing alone, and should negotiate to receive at least $290M in total value from the arrangement.

## Outcome

Excavator alone generates $120M total value ($300M profit minus $180M capital investment). Drillhammer alone generates -$200M total value (loses $60M over life plus $140M capital). The JV generates $460M total value ($800M profit minus $340M capital investment), creating $340M in incremental value. Recommended split: Drillhammer receives $170M, Excavator receives $290M.

## Discussion Questions

- What are the investment alternatives available to each company, and how should their next best alternatives influence the JV negotiation?
- Is a 50-50 equal split of the $460M JV value fair to both parties, and what would be a more equitable arrangement?
- How sensitive is the investment decision to changes in the assumed copper ore price of $50/tonne over the 20-year mine life?
