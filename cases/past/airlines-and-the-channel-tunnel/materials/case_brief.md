## Current Situation

The Channel Tunnel created a new competitive dynamic on the London-Paris route by offering an alternative to air travel. Airlines had previously dominated this route and relied heavily on business passengers as their highest-margin customer segment. The rail service presented a direct competitor on the same city-pair route, forcing airlines to reassess their market position and strategy.

## Key Challenges

Following the opening of the Channel Tunnel connecting Britain and France by train, airlines operating the London-Paris route faced significant competitive pressure from rail service. The case examines how airlines should respond to this threat, particularly regarding business passengers who represented their most profitable market segment.
