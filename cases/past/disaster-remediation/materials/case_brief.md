# Case Brief: Disaster Remediation

**Company:** US-based fire and water remediation services provider
**Industry:** services
**Source:** Haas_2006.pdf (pages 39–40)
**Tags:** market_entry, services, profitability_analysis, competitive_positioning, diversification

---

## Current Situation

The client is a US-based provider of fire and water remediation services, primarily hired by insurance companies to clean properties damaged by fire or flooding. This core business is highly profitable. The US residential cleaning market is approximately $50 billion with stable 4% annual growth. The market is fragmented: national players control 10% of the market, regional players 20%, and independent individuals 60-70%. Customer preferences vary by segment, with quality-conscious buyers preferring national players and price-conscious buyers preferring individuals.

## Key Challenges

A profitable fire and water remediation company is considering expanding into the US residential cleaning market. The company must evaluate whether entering this large but competitive market aligns with its capabilities and whether it can compete effectively against established players.

## Decision

The company decided to enter the US residential cleaning market, leveraging its existing customer service reputation, client contacts, and operational expertise, while targeting the quality-conscious customer segment.

## Outcome

Preliminary financial analysis shows the client could achieve a 27% profit margin ($75 price - $50 labor and supply costs = $18.75 profit per job) competing against national players in the quality segment. However, the outcome of actual market entry was not disclosed; the case indicates the client must develop a tailored value proposition and anticipate competitor reactions before proceeding.

## Discussion Questions

- How should the company position itself differently from national competitors like Dial-A-Maid when entering the residential cleaning market?
- What are the key differences between the insurance-based sales model for remediation services and the direct-to-consumer sales model required for residential cleaning, and how should the company adapt?
