## Current Situation

The fast food chain had acquired several new locations and replicated its existing business model across them. The firm's older stores were located in predominantly lower-income neighborhoods where they had established market presence. The company expanded into new markets but faced unexpected performance disparities, with some new stores performing well while others struggled financially despite higher per-customer sales volumes.

## Key Challenges

A fast food chain experiencing rapid growth through store acquisitions found that new locations were significantly underperforming compared to established stores, despite selling more product per customer. The chain needed to understand why new stores were attracting far fewer customers and barely breaking even while older stores thrived.

## Decision

Not specified

## Outcome

Analysis revealed that location and competitive positioning were critical factors. The firm's established stores operated in low-income areas with minimal competition, while new stores were primarily in upscale malls with higher competition. The firm's low-price brand positioning was a competitive disadvantage in upscale markets, explaining the customer attraction gap.

## Discussion Questions

- What are the key differences between the performing and underperforming stores in terms of revenue drivers versus cost structure?
- How do the store locations and competitive landscapes differ between the original stores and the newly acquired locations?
- What adjustments to pricing, product mix, or brand positioning would be needed to succeed in upscale mall locations?
