# Case Brief: Purple Pill Company

**Company:** Multinational pharmaceutical and biologics company (unnamed)
**Industry:** healthcare
**Source:** Fuqua-Case-book-2014.pdf (pages 15–22)
**Tags:** patent_expiration, generics, pharmaceutical, pricing_strategy, product_portfolio, revenue_decline, otc, direct_to_consumer

## Current Situation

The company has a diversified portfolio across major disease areas including cancer, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, neuroscience, and respiratory. Current revenues total approximately $21B. The Green Pill (neuroscience) has declined 40% year-over-year, Red Pill (cardiovascular) declined 8%, while Blue Pill (respiratory) grew 10%. The Purple Pill generates $3.2B annually (15% of portfolio) with $1 revenue per pill at 80% margin, with over two-thirds of revenue coming from the US market.

## Key Challenges

A multinational pharmaceutical company is experiencing an 18-month revenue decline and faces the imminent patent expiration of its most commercially successful drug (Purple Pill for acid reflux) in May of the following year. The company needs to identify areas to reverse the revenue decline and determine how to maintain as much revenue as possible from the Purple Pill after patent expiration.

## Decision

Not specified; candidates must evaluate three options: (1) do nothing, (2) license the brand to an OTC manufacturer for $250M one-time revenue, or (3) launch a direct-to-patient online campaign with third-party delivery
