# Case Brief: Insurance Company

**Company:** Unnamed major insurance company
**Industry:** finance
**Source:** Columbia_2006.pdf (pages 80–82)
**Tags:** growth_strategy, market_entry, insurance, quantitative_analysis, private_market, revenue_growth

---

## Current Situation

The company currently insures 1 million people through corporate accounts, generating $2 billion in premium revenues and $200 million in net revenues (10% margin). The market serves 10 million company-insured individuals out of 20 million total in the region. Key market trends include declining corporate insurance offerings and a growing 65+ demographic segment. The company holds a 10% market share in the company-insured segment.

## Key Challenges

A major insurance company selling corporate packages in the West Pacific Rim faces slowing growth from traditional channels (new accounts and within-account expansion) as companies cut back on employee insurance programs. The CEO seeks new growth opportunities to increase revenues.

## Decision

The company should enter the privately insured market segment, targeting 100,000 new customers over three years to capture the incremental $20 million profit growth required (10% growth from current $200 million base).

## Outcome

The analysis concludes this is feasible if the private insurance market expands from 600,000 to 1 million people over three years (400,000 net switch from public to private insurance) and the company maintains its 10% market share. Quantitative targets: 100,000 new privately insured customers, $20 million incremental profits in 3 years, requiring $2,000 average premium per customer.

## Discussion Questions

- What options can the company consider to spur growth beyond traditional account expansion?
- Before entering the privately insured market, what factors should the company evaluate regarding market conditions, competition, and operations?
- If the company needs $20 million in incremental profits over three years from the private insurance market, what market size and company market share assumptions are required?
