# Case Brief: Gas Stations and Convenience Stores

**Industry:** energy

## Current Situation

The company operates an integrated value chain from oil rigs through refining, distribution, and retail. Globally, the number of gas stations has declined 6% over 15 years due to consolidation and operational efficiencies. However, the market structure is shifting: while convenience stores attached to gas stations are increasing, supermarkets have entered the market aggressively. Supermarket gas stations now control 10% of the US market, 30% in the UK, and 60% in France, suggesting a superior business model.

## Key Challenges

A major global oil company's retail operation—comprising gas station pumps and convenience stores—has experienced declining profitability. The CEO needs to understand the root causes and develop a five-year recovery strategy in the face of market changes including consolidation and new competition from supermarket gas stations.

## Decision

After analyzing ROIC metrics and identifying that convenience store profitability—not fuel sales—drives returns, the client decided to launch a pilot program to expand convenience store offerings across 1,000 locations, with a focus on introducing high-margin, frequently-purchased items requiring minimal shelf space.

## Outcome

The company selected hot and cold food products for introduction, chosen for their high margins, low shelf-space requirements, and high purchase frequency. McKinsey was implementing the program in European locations at the time of the case writing. Final financial outcomes not disclosed.

## Discussion Questions

- Why have supermarkets been able to achieve a superior ROIC (24%) compared to traditional gas stations, and what are the structural barriers to closing this gap?
- Given that convenience store profitability—not fuel margins—drives returns, what is the optimal product mix and pricing strategy for convenience stores to maximize ROIC?
