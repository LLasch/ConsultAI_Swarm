# CASE BRIEF: Ferrari Strategic Transition Analysis

## 1. Client Overview

**Ferrari N.V.** - Italian luxury performance automotive manufacturer
- **Core Business**: Design, engineering, production and sale of luxury performance sports cars
- **Mission**: Maintain exclusivity while pursuing controlled growth strategy
- **Heritage**: Founded on Formula 1 racing legacy with technology transfer from track to road cars
- **Production**: Two facilities in Italy (Modena for aluminum/carbon bodies, Maranello for engines/assembly)
- **Manufacturing Capacity**: 89 engines/day average in 2023, employing 1,797 engineers/technicians/production workers

## 2. Current Situation

### Financial & Operational Metrics
- **Production Growth**: Hybrid vehicles grew from 2% (2020) → 15% (2021) → 51% (2024) of unit shipments
- **Workforce**: 1,797 manufacturing employees (93% Italian, 92% male, 4.1% annual attrition)
- **Employee Satisfaction**: 71% would recommend to friend, 79% positive outlook, 81% CEO approval
- **Supply Chain**: 70,000+ unique components from ~500 suppliers (top 10 = 20% of procurement costs)
- **Single Sourcing**: No alternative sources qualified for most components; limited long-term supplier agreements

### Market Context
- **Luxury EV Market**: Expected growth from 7,000 units (2021) → 280,000 units (2031)
- **Regulatory Pressure**: EU mandate for zero CO2 emissions by 2035 (exemption for <1,000 units/year producers)
- **Infrastructure Gaps**: Europe needs 9M charging points by 2030 (requires 8x historical installation rate)

## 3. Key Challenges

1. **Electric Vehicle Transition Uncertainty**
   - Customer demand weaker than expected at luxury price points
   - Used hybrid values declining (SF90: 20-25% discount, 296 GTB showing value drops)

2. **Technology Capability Gap**
   - Limited in-house expertise in EV components (batteries, electric motors, software, power electronics)
   - Must balance external sourcing with preserving technological know-how

3. **Market Demand Volatility**
   - Competitor delays: Aston Martin postponed EV launch 2025→2026, Bentley CEO notes luxury customers "reject electric cars"
   - Range anxiety and charging infrastructure limitations persist

4. **Controlled Growth Strategy Risks**
   - Entering new markets while maintaining exclusivity
   - Managing new business risks without proven expertise/systems
   - Balancing customer preferences across ICE/hybrid/electric portfolio

5. **Competitive Positioning**
   - Formula E absence while competitors (Maserati, Jaguar, McLaren, Porsche) participate
   - Technology-neutral stance may limit market influence

## 4. Stakeholder Perspectives

**CEO Benedetto Vigna:**
> "The e-building is not just about 'electric.' It will allow us to put all the pieces of the puzzle together so that the entire research and development and manufacturing process is optimal...Ferrari is faster moving than a traditional automotive company, but slower than a technology company."

> "We remain focused on Formula 1, because that's where we see a lot of traction. Formula E's...appeal is not so strong."

**Aston Martin Executive Chairman (competitor insight):**
> "The consumer demand [for electric vehicles], certainly at an Aston Martin price point, is not what we thought it was going to be two years ago."

**Bentley CEO Frank-Steffan Walliser:**
> "What we see in the luxury market right now [is that] people reject electric cars. They consider luxury cars only with the combustion engine."

**Industry Analyst:**
> "Electric vehicles have rapidly evolved from being a curiosity to a disruptive force. They're upending the relationship between car manufacturers and dealerships, redefining the future of automotive service, and creating customer experience challenges."

## 5. Financial Highlights

### Portfolio Targets
- **2026 Goal**: 55% hybrid, 40% ICE, 5% fully electric
- **2030 Goal**: 40% hybrid, 40% fully electric, 20% ICE

### Customer Behavior Data
- **UHNW Customers**: 67% reducing carbon footprint, 40% planning EV switch
- **ICE Luxury Owners**: 18% plan hybrid purchase, 44% plan electric purchase
- **Historical Performance**: LaFerrari (2013): 399 units at $1M+ each

### Market Benchmarks
- **Tesla Roadster**: $129,000, 0-60 mph in 3.7 seconds (2008 baseline)
- **Ferrari Hybrid Performance**: SF90 Stradale 0-60 mph in 2.4 seconds, 950 HP

## 6. Constraints

### Regulatory
- EU CO2 emissions mandate by 2035 (Ferrari likely exempt due to <1,000 units/year)
- Various country ICE bans starting 2025

### Operational
- Single-source supplier dependencies with no alternatives qualified
- Limited long-term supplier agreements
- Technology neutral stance limits market influence capability

### Infrastructure
- Charging network inadequacy (concentrated in Netherlands, France, Germany)
- High-temperature battery management challenges for supercars

### Timeline
- First fully electric model launch: 2025
- E-building operational: 2024 (just opened)
