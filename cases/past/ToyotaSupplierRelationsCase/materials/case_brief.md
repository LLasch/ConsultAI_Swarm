# Case Brief: ToyotaSupplierRelationsCase

# CASE BRIEF: Toyota Supplier Relations - Fixing the Suprima Chassis

## 1. Client Overview

**Toyota Motor Manufacturing Georgia (TMMGA)** - Major automotive assembly plant in Macon, Georgia
- **Mission**: Produce Toyota's Suprima crossover vehicle for North American market using Toyota Production System (TPS)
- **Operations**: Award-winning assembly plant, part of Toyota's expanding North American network (expected 8 assembly plants + 5 engine plants by 2007)
- **Market Position**: Toyota valued at $200+ billion, recognized for operational excellence and TPS implementation
- **Philosophy**: Built on "continuous improvement and respect for people" - treating all stakeholders (employees, suppliers, customers, dealers, shareholders) with mutual respect

## 2. Current Situation

**ChassisCo Athens Plant Performance (November 2004)**:
- **Production Rate**: Only 60% of target capacity
- **Quality Issues**: Frequent nonconforming parts, inadequate problem tracking
- **Timeline**: 14 months into production with insufficient improvement
- **Facility**: 120,000 sq ft plant, 350+ workforce, producing ~200,000 rear suspension cradles/year
- **Operations**: 27 daily shipments, serving Toyota exclusively for Suprima model

**Operational Changes from 1997 Model**:
- Robots increased: 13 → 102 units
- Welding work doubled per cradle
- Managing 85 parts in rear suspension cradle
- Quality staff expanded: 18 → 90 people
- Added 2 warehouses for parts storage
- Factory footprint reduced by 50% while doubling value-added content

## 3. Key Challenges

1. **Production Performance Gap**: Standard production rate at only 60% of target despite 14 months of operation
2. **Quality Control Failures**: Rear suspension cradles consistently out of conformance with frequent nonconforming parts
3. **Problem Detection & Tracking**: Inadequate systems to identify and track quality issues
4. **System Integration Issues**: Entire supply and build system lacks sufficient control and improvement mechanisms
5. **Resource Utilization**: Despite massive investment in automation and quality staff, performance targets not met
6. **Program Risk**: Entire Suprima vehicle program potentially at risk due to chassis quality issues

## 4. Stakeholder Perspectives

**Walt Bernstein (Toyota Director of Production Control)**:
> "This situation is one of the most challenging I have faced in my 20 years with Toyota... We've utilized all of our supplier support resources to solve the problems, but we still have not made adequate headway."

> "If we don't turn this around, the entire vehicle program could be at risk. Quality could be impacted. We need to identify and address any potential quality and conformance issues immediately."

**David McDonald (ChassisCo Plant Manager)**:
> "We're working as hard as we can... my staff and I are working 12 hours a day, seven days a week. What more do you want me to do?"

> "We have taken responsibility for the development and supply of virtually every one of the 85 parts... and we aggressively worked the prices on second tier parts supply to meet the extremely aggressive target price demanded by Toyota."

**Senior Japanese Toyota Executive** (on TPS implementation):
> "Learning the Toyota Production System is a little like learning to play golf... it is simple to explain, but mastering it is a challenge over a lifetime."

## 5. Financial Highlights

**Investment Scale**:
- Massive automation upgrade: 13 → 102 robots
- Quality staff expansion: 18 → 90 people (400% increase)
- Added 2 warehouses for parts storage
- Factory footprint utilization: 50% reduction while doubling output capacity

**Performance Metrics**:
- Current production rate: 60% of target
- Annual output target: ~200,000 rear suspension cradles
- Daily shipment frequency: 27 shipments
- Production duration: 14 months with insufficient improvement trajectory

**Cost Pressures**:
- "Extremely aggressive target price demanded by Toyota"
- Doubled value-added work content in reduced footprint
- Price pressure on second-tier parts supply

## 6. Constraints

**Timeline Pressure**: 14 months in production with urgent need for resolution
**Quality Requirements**: Zero tolerance for nonconforming parts given Toyota's quality standards
**Cost Constraints**: Aggressive target pricing with doubled work content requirements
**Resource Limitations**: Already deployed "all supplier support resources" including multiple Toyota expert teams
**Operational Constraints**: 
- Single customer/product dependency (Toyota Suprima only)
- Complex 85-part assembly management
- Integration of 102 robots requiring coordination

**Regulatory/Standards**: Toyota Production System compliance requirements
**Organizational**: Staff working 12-hour days, 7 days/week - sustainability concerns
