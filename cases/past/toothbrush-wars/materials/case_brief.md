# Case Brief: Toothbrush Wars

**Company:** Global consumer products company (toothbrush division)
**Industry:** consumer
**Source:** Haas_2006.pdf (pages 32–33)
**Tags:** pricing_strategy, product_cannibalization, market_entry, disruptive_technology, product_portfolio, competitive_response, margin_analysis

---

## Current Situation

The client is a division of a global consumer products company with two main toothbrush offerings: manual toothbrushes at $3 (80% market share) and rechargeable toothbrushes at $50 (20% market share). The rechargeable segment is highly profitable, generating $12 profit per customer per year through a two-component model (base and heads). The manual segment generates $4 profit per customer per year. A competitor's spinbrush at $5 has disrupted the market, targeting the gap between manual and rechargeable segments.

## Key Challenges

A new competitor has introduced a battery-powered spinbrush retailing at $5 that has captured 1% market share, primarily cannibilizing the client's high-margin rechargeable toothbrush sales ($50). The client must decide whether to develop a comparable spinbrush product to compete or pursue alternative strategies.

## Discussion Questions

- Should the client develop a spinbrush product to compete directly with the competitor's offering, or would this accelerate the decline of its profitable rechargeable business?
- What alternative competitive responses could the client pursue to defend its rechargeable toothbrush segment without directly competing on the spinbrush product?
- How should the client prioritize protecting high-margin rechargeable sales versus defending market share in the mass-market manual segment?
