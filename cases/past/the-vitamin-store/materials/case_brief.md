# Case Brief: The Vitamin Store

**Company:** The Vitamin Store
**Industry:** retail
**Source:** LBS_2006.pdf (pages 82–88)
**Tags:** private_equity, valuation, retail, market_entry, cost_structure, competitive_analysis, acquisition

---

## Current Situation

The Vitamin Store is a retailer specializing in vitamins, minerals, natural medicines, sports supplements, herbs, dietary supplements, and nutrition products. The market is approximately $11-13 billion as of 2002. The competitive landscape includes independents (>10,000 stores, representing 68-70% market share), new specialist supermarkets like Whole Foods (9-15% market share), and large retailers like Walmart and Tesco (15-23% market share). The Vitamin Store appears to be losing market share to larger competitors despite industry growth driven by demographics, health trends, and obesity concerns.

## Key Challenges

A private equity firm is evaluating whether to acquire The Vitamin Store, a health and nutrition products retailer. The firm needs to determine the company's valuation, assess market dynamics, and understand the competitive positioning to decide if the acquisition represents a sound investment.

## Discussion Questions

- What was The Vitamin Store's market share in 2002, and what does its share loss relative to independents suggest about its customer base and competitive positioning?
- How should the private equity firm address the wide variation in profitability across The Vitamin Store's locations—should it improve underperforming stores or divest them?
- What are The Vitamin Store's cost structure advantages or disadvantages compared to large retailers like Walmart and Whole Foods in terms of supplier relationships and real estate productivity?
