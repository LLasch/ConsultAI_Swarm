# Case Brief: Chinese Cars

**Company:** Large Chinese conglomerate (unnamed)
**Industry:** manufacturing
**Source:** Columbia_2006.pdf (pages 9–11)
**Tags:** market_entry, china, automotive, supply_chain, b2b, manufacturing, export

---

## Current Situation

The Chinese company has significant cash reserves and has invested in a new aluminum wheel manufacturing plant ready for production by end of year, with planned capacity of 8 million wheels annually. Management intends to sell 20% domestically in China and seek the remaining 80% in the US, which is the world's largest vehicle market. The company engaged Roland Berger to evaluate market feasibility. The US automotive market produces approximately 17 million vehicles annually, with 70% equipped with aluminum wheels, and there is an aftermarket of 85 million vehicles with 24 million less than 5 years old.

## Key Challenges

A Chinese conglomerate has built an aluminum wheel production plant with 8 million units annual capacity and wants to determine if it can sell 80% of production (6.4 million units) to the US market. The core question is whether this aggressive export target is feasible given US market dynamics and industry structure.

## Decision

The company decided to subcontract production capacity to suppliers of major US car manufacturers rather than pursuing direct sales to OEMs or the aftermarket. This approach allows entry into the US market at scale by leveraging existing supplier relationships, though with reduced profit margins.

## Outcome

The company implemented the subcontracting strategy as recommended. This was identified as the most efficient way to enter the US market on a large scale given the constraints of OEM procurement timelines (24 months supplier selection plus 12 months to get on preferred supplier list = 3 years lead time) and the company's immediate production readiness.

## Discussion Questions

- What are the key barriers to entering the US automotive supply market, and how do they vary between the OEM and aftermarket channels?
- Is the company's production capacity realistic relative to addressable market size, and what would be required to capture sufficient market share?
