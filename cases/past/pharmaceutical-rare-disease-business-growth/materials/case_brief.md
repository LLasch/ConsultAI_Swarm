# Case Brief: Pharmaceutical Rare Disease Business Growth

**Company:** Large pharmaceutical company (unnamed)
**Industry:** healthcare
**Source:** Fuqua-Case-book-2014.pdf (pages 244–249)
**Tags:** growth_strategy, acquisitions, pharmaceutical, rare_disease, m&a

---

## Current Situation

The client is an industry leader in rare disease pharmaceuticals with a current business of $2.5B expected to grow organically at 5% per year. Rare diseases affect fewer than 200,000 people in the US. Historically, pharmaceutical companies avoided this market due to cost ineffectiveness, but recent regulatory improvements (government tax credits) and the decline in blockbuster drug pipelines have made rare disease R&D more viable. The overall rare disease market is valued at $30B and is as profitable as traditional Big Pharma, with growth rates exceeding the broader pharmaceutical sector.

## Key Challenges

A large pharmaceutical company with a $2.5B rare disease business wants to double its revenue within 5 years. The company must determine the optimal strategy to achieve this growth target in a market that was historically underserved but is now becoming attractive due to regulatory changes and improved R&D economics.

## Decision

Pursue a mixed strategy combining internal growth with strategic acquisitions. Acquire approximately 3 'peak revenue' assets to supplement organic growth and close the gap to the $5B target revenue goal.

## Outcome

Internal growth alone would generate only $1.1B by 2016, creating a $1.4B shortfall from the $2.5B doubling goal. Three strategic acquisitions of rare disease assets are required to achieve the target. The case does not disclose whether this strategy was actually implemented or what the final results were.

## Discussion Questions

- How would you approach the client's goal of doubling its Rare Disease business in 5 years?
- What is the gap between organic growth projections and the doubling target, and what strategies could close it?
- Should the client acquire small biotechs, assets from competing pharmaceutical companies, or pursue a combination, and how many acquisitions are needed?
