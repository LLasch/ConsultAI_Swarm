## Current Situation

The private equity firm currently operates exclusively in the automotive industry. The automotive sensor market shows strong growth potential, with sensors expected to represent approximately $150 per vehicle by 2005 and electronics content growing to 17% of total purchased materials. The market is highly fragmented with leading companies pursuing growth through acquisitions and partnerships. The sensor business requires significant continuing R&D investment but offers high margins expected to persist for 4-5 years.

## Key Challenges

A private equity firm operating primarily in the automotive industry must decide whether to expand their portfolio into the sensor market and establish criteria for identifying acquisition targets. The firm needs to evaluate the investment opportunity using financial analysis and strategic fit considerations.

## Decision

Based on the NPV analysis and strategic framework presented, the client should pursue investment in the sensor market. The analysis reveals two key success criteria: profit/margin growth potential and fit with existing portfolio.

## Outcome

NPV analysis indicates a positive return of $1.34M over three years at 10% cost of capital, with cumulative cash flows of $31.26M against total investments of $29.92M. The framework identifies multiple strategic advantages including access to new technologies, portfolio diversification, and potential industry leadership in a fragmented market.

## Discussion Questions

- What are the two primary criteria for evaluating sensor company acquisition targets, and how should each be assessed during due diligence?
- How should the firm balance the advantages of market entry (new technologies, diversification, leadership potential) against the disadvantages (acquisition costs, R&D requirements, learning curve, longer product development cycles)?
