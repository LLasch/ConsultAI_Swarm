## Current Situation

This is a market sizing case from McKinsey & Company's 2005 Summer final round. The case focuses on the piano market in the United States, which serves two primary customer segments: personal households and institutions (colleges, universities, concert halls, businesses). The case emphasizes that the methodology and logical reasoning behind the estimate are more important than arriving at exact numbers.

## Key Challenges

Estimate the total annual revenue from piano sales in the United States. The candidate must develop a logical market sizing framework by identifying customer segments, estimating purchase behavior, and calculating average prices.

## Decision

The example dialogue demonstrates a systematic approach: segmenting buyers (households vs. institutions), estimating household income levels and ownership rates, calculating annual purchase rates by segment, and then determining average prices for both new and used pianos.

## Outcome

The example calculation arrives at approximately $2.2 billion in total annual piano sales ($1B in new piano sales and $1.2B in used piano sales), based on 510K total pianos sold annually at blended average prices.

## Discussion Questions

- What are the key customer segments for piano purchases and how would you estimate the size of each?
- How would you estimate the percentage of target households by income level, and what current piano ownership rates would you assume?
- What percentage of piano sales would you expect to be new versus used, and how would this differ between household and institutional buyers?
