# Case Brief: Tulsa Hotel - OK or not OK?

**Company:** Tulsa Hotel
**Industry:** hospitality
**Source:** Wharton_2010.pdf (pages 70–73)
**Tags:** hospitality, investment_decision, pricing_strategy, profitability_analysis, revenue_growth

---

## Current Situation

The hotel has 500 rooms and operates with different occupancy rates and pricing on weekends versus weekdays. Weekend occupancy is 60% with a mix of individual guests ($150/room) and group guests ($120/room). Weekday occupancy is 75% with similar guest mix. Fixed costs are $5,750/day and variable costs are $30/occupied room/night. The financial projections show annual profits of approximately $11M.

## Key Challenges

A client is considering a $20M investment in a Tulsa hotel and expects a 20% ROI over three years. The case asks whether this investment is financially justified based on profitability analysis, and then explores strategies to increase revenues while holding costs constant.

## Decision

Yes, invest in the hotel. The analysis shows projected 3-year profits of $33M, which exceeds the required $12M threshold to achieve the 20% ROI target on the $20M investment.

## Outcome

Projected 3-year profit of $33M against a required profit of $12M to meet 20% ROI target ($33M > $12M, therefore investment is recommended). Average revenue per room per night calculated at $130 across all days.

## Discussion Questions

- Should the client make the $20M investment given the profitability projections?
- What is the average revenue per customer per day for any day of the week?
- What strategies could increase revenues while holding costs constant?
