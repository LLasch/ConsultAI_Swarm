# Case Brief: Street Sweepers

**Company:** Industrial conglomerate (street sweeper manufacturing unit)
**Industry:** manufacturing
**Source:** Columbia_2006.pdf (pages 44–47)
**Tags:** market_share_loss, product_innovation, competitive_response, cost_management, manufacturing, turnaround, divestiture, market_segmentation

---

## Current Situation

The conglomerate generates $60-70 million in annual revenues and is highly profitable overall. The street sweeper unit has operated for 20 years and was historically profitable, but began experiencing margin pressure 4-5 years ago. Approximately 5 years ago, a competitor (B) introduced new air vacuum technology that is more effective and efficient than the traditional mechanical sweeping methods, causing the client to lose market share. The client still maintains a portion of the market despite this competition.

## Key Challenges

A street sweeper manufacturing unit within a large industrial conglomerate has experienced declining profit margins over the past 5 years and is now near breakeven. Revenue has fallen steadily despite constant costs and prices, and management is unwilling to make significant capital investments. BCG must analyze the unit's performance and recommend a path forward.

## Decision

BCG recommended that the company protect its remaining market share in the mechanical sweeper segment by scaling back production to equilibrium levels where both mechanical and air sweepers coexist based on regional needs. The conglomerate subsequently sold the unit to a foreign company that already possessed air technology.

## Outcome

The unit was divested to a strategic buyer with existing air technology capabilities. The analysis identified that approximately 64% of the national market would remain mechanical sweepers (ranging from 22% in the south to 22% in the north, with air sweepers at 18-8% respectively), indicating a stable niche market could be maintained with proper positioning.

## Discussion Questions

- Why did competitor B's air vacuum technology capture market share despite similar pricing to mechanical sweepers?
- What are the key differences in customer needs between northern and southern regions that affect the mechanical vs. air sweeper mix?
